The COVID-19 pandemic in the United States was characterized by a partisan gap: Democrats were more concerned about the health threat, more willing to socially distance, and more likely to support mitigating policies than Republicans. In cross-sectional analyses of three nationally representative survey waves in 2020, we find that adverse experience with COVID-19 is associated with a narrowing of the partisan gap, though analyses of longitudinal and county-level data do not show a consistent relationship. We find consistent results for self-reported experience of extreme weather events and climate change attitudes, suggesting personal experience may be an important channel for concern about novel threats.
